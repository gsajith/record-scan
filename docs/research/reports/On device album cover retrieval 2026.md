# Train the query encoder, shrink the index

> Written against the 2025 gallery of 3,128,310 covers. The 2026-10-25 catalog update raised it to 3,977,195, so size and cost figures here run about 1.27x low until Task 5's deduplicated count replaces both. D10 now keeps the original covers instead of 256 px copies, and makes any resized copy for the GPU upload after Task 7 picks an input size (plan, 2026-10-27).

Record Scan should drop frozen CLIP ViT-B/32 and its 13.6 augmented vectors per cover, store one vector per deduplicated cover, and put all tolerance for glare, skew and crop error into a query encoder trained on synthetic phone photos. The backbone to beat is DINOv3: its 21M-parameter ViT-S/16 scores 49.5 mAP on Revisited Oxford-Hard, ahead of every SigLIP 2 and Perception Encoder model up to giant size, and frozen DINOv3-B scores 26.4 on mini-ILIAS against 5.9 for OpenAI CLIP-B. Very little of that is new since November 2025. DINOv3, SigLIP 2, Perception Encoder and MobileCLIP2 all shipped before then. The eleven months since added evidence and tooling instead: an October 2026 paper showing that object-to-frame ratio mismatch, not resolution, drives most query failures, measured phone latencies for ViT-B-class encoders at 10 to 21 ms, ExecuTorch 1.0 and coremltools 9, and a crowd of subscription scanner apps that leave a free, fast app an opening. The recommended system is hybrid at first. A distilled 20 to 30M-parameter student runs on the phone and sends a 256-d vector, about 512 bytes, to a CPU server that searches the full gallery. Search moves onto the phone only if a product-quantized index of about 0.2 GB keeps top-1 accuracy within 2 points of exact search on real photos. Training costs tens of dollars of rented GPU time, and the M1 handles evaluation, ablations and export. Every major choice in the plan below is gated on one asset the project does not have yet: a gold set of a few hundred real phone photos plus "no record" frames.

## Eleven months brought better evidence, not better backbones

The November 2025 baseline was built on the weakest of the encoders that already existed. **No new general-purpose backbone aimed at instance retrieval appeared between November 2025 and October 2026.** The DINOv3 repo's post-November news is a canopy-height model (March 2026) and metadata-guided training recipes (June 2026) ([dinov3 README](https://github.com/facebookresearch/dinov3)). Perception Encoder's only addition is audio-visual models from December 2025 ([perception_models README](https://github.com/facebookresearch/perception_models)). MobileCLIP2, released 28 August 2025, is still Apple's newest family ([ml-mobileclip README](https://github.com/apple/ml-mobileclip)). The one major new release, TIPSv2 at CVPR 2026, starts at B/14 (85.7M) and reports no Oxford, Paris or ILIAS numbers ([google-deepmind/tips](https://github.com/google-deepmind/tips)). So the backbone question is settled by models from mid-2025 that the project simply never tried.

What did change is the evidence about how to use them and the tooling to ship them. The table sorts the developments that bear on this project.

The product context moved too. Discogs and CLZ Music still identify records by barcode and text, not cover photo ([Discogs iOS help](https://support.discogs.com/hc/en-us/articles/360014043894-How-To-Search-In-The-Database-When-Using-The-Discogs-iOS-App); [CLZ Music](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/clz-music-cd-vinyl-database/id317997093)). Nobody has published a phone-photo-to-cover benchmark, so Record Scan's own gold set is the only scoreboard that exists ([GitHub project-33t](https://github.com/boehm-s/project-33t)).

## DINOv3 leads instance retrieval by 2 to 4x, but text-heavy covers keep a VLM in the race

The published numbers put the current backbone near the bottom of the modern options. On ILIAS, a 2025 instance-retrieval benchmark with up to 100M distractors, **frozen OpenAI CLIP ViT-B/16 scores 5.9 mAP@1k on the 5M mini set, against 26.4 for DINOv3-B and 18.4 for SigLIP2-B@384** ([ILIAS](https://vrg.fel.cvut.cz/ilias/)). The project uses ViT-B/32, which has coarser patches and is unlikely to do better than B/16. On Revisited Oxford-Hard, DINOv3's distilled family scores 49.5 (S, 21M), 50.0 (S+, 29M) and 58.5 (B, 86M), while SigLIP 2 B and PE-Core B both sit at 20.2 ([DINOv3, Table 14](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.10104)). On the Met, the artwork benchmark closest to album covers, the DINOv3 7B teacher scores 55.4 GAP against 13.9 for SigLIP 2 g/16 ([DINOv3, Table 9](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.10104)). DINOv3 also holds up better as the gallery grows: DINOv3-B falls from 26.4 to 22.0 between 5M and 100M distractors, while PE-B falls from 20.2 to 9.7 ([ILIAS](https://vrg.fel.cvut.cz/ilias/)). A 3.13M gallery sits in the range where that difference shows.

The strongest case against DINOv3 is that album covers are not landmarks. They are flat, printed and often full of type, closer to packaged products. On Products-10k, text-image models beat self-supervised ones in a 2025 e-commerce benchmark, 0.581 to 0.428 frozen and 0.641 to 0.465 after top-tuning ([arXiv 2504.07567](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.07567)). Light adaptation also helps VLMs far more than DINO. A linear layer trained on UnED lifts SigLIP2-B@384 from 18.4 to 27.5 and PE-L@336 from 27.1 to 39.6, while DINOv3-B stays at 26.4 ([ILIAS](https://vrg.fel.cvut.cz/ilias/)). An adapted SigLIP 2 B can therefore match or pass a frozen DINOv3-B. My judgment is that DINOv3 is the favorite but not the certain winner. The first sweep must include one VLM and must score text-only covers as a separate slice.

The DINOv3 License grants a royalty-free right to use, distribute and modify the weights with no commercial prohibition and no user-count threshold. Shipping the weights in an app counts as distribution, so the app must include a copy of the license, and the license bans trade-control end uses and reverse engineering and ends on breach ([DINOv3 LICENSE.md](https://github.com/facebookresearch/dinov3/blob/main/LICENSE.md)). That fits a free consumer app with an acknowledgements screen. MobileCLIP2 has the best published phone latency, but its weights fall under Apple's ML Research Model terms ([ml-mobileclip README](https://github.com/apple/ml-mobileclip)). The research did not read that text, and if it limits use to research, MobileCLIP2 cannot ship. Treat it as a latency reference until someone reads the license on the Hugging Face card. PE-Core S/16 is the Apache-2.0 fallback for a small VLM.

Resolution is the open variable. ILIAS ran DINOv3 at 768 px ([ILIAS](https://vrg.fel.cvut.cz/ilias/)), and a phone student will run at 224 to 256 px. ViT attention cost grows with the square of the token count, so 448 px costs roughly 4x the attention compute of 224 px, while ConvNeXt scales linearly. Nobody has published DINOv3 retrieval at 224 px or ConvNeXt retrieval at any size, so the sweep has to measure both.

## One vector per cover, trained SSCD-style, replaces 42.5M augmented vectors

The baseline's index-time augmentation does not scale. At 13.6 vectors per image the full gallery is 42.5M vectors, about 43 GB on disk and roughly 100 GB of RAM for an hnswlib graph at M=48, which does not fit in 64 GB ([recovery plan, B3](../../superpowers/plans/2026-10-24-record-scan-recovery.md)). The literature points the other way. **Every strong instance-retrieval and copy-detection result since 2021 trains the query-side embedding on heavy, task-specific augmentation and indexes one clean vector per reference.** The Meta Image Similarity Challenge organizers found that "strong and non-standard image augmentations that mimic typical cases of image copies are very beneficial" ([ISC 2021](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2202.04007)). In the Google Universal Image Embedding challenge, fine-tuning lifted CLIP ViT-H/14 from 62.1 to 72.8 mP@5 ([GUIE](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.01858)).

Two loss families won those competitions: ArcFace on a small projection, and instance-contrastive InfoNCE with an entropy term. For Record Scan, InfoNCE fits better. Each cover is its own class with one image, and ArcFace over 3.13M classes needs a 3.13M x 512 weight matrix of about 6.4 GB at fp32. The SSCD recipe is the template: GeM pooling, one linear projection, L2 norm, PCA whitening, loss InfoNCE plus a KoLeo differential-entropy term with λ = 30 (unstable above 40), temperature 0.05 and a large batch ([SSCD, Pizzi et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.10261)). The entropy term alone added 17.4 µAP in SSCD's ablation, and stronger augmentation added about 6 more (50.4 to 56.8) ([SSCD](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.10261)). The ISC descriptor winner fit large-batch negatives on limited hardware with a cross-batch memory bank ([ISC 2021](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2202.04007)). For the schedule, the GUIE winner trained the head first and then the backbone with the head frozen, which scored the same as LP-FT (0.678 against 0.677) and is cheap protection for pretrained features ([Shao and Cui](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.08473)).

Three adaptations make the recipe fit covers. First, make the pairs asymmetric. The anchor is the clean cover and the positive is a synthetic photo of it, which matches deployment better than SimCLR's two augmented views. Second, deduplicate before training. Many releases share one cover, and treating identical art as negatives pushes identical images apart. Cluster covers by perceptual hash and teacher embedding, collapse each cluster to one index entry that lists its releases, and exclude same-cluster items from the negatives. Third, mine hard negatives with the teacher's nearest neighbors across release groups, because text-only and minimalist covers are the expected failure slice.

No published augmentation set targets camera captures of physical sleeves. Copy-detection sets cover blur, crops, rotation, text and emoji overlays, JPEG and mixup ([SSCD Table 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.10261)), so the phone-specific terms have to come from library transforms. Albumentations' Perspective defaults to a corner displacement scale of 0.05 to 0.1 of image size ([Albumentations](https://albumentations.ai/docs/api-reference/albumentations/augmentations/geometric/transforms/)). Its maintained fork, AlbumentationsX, ships RandomSunFlare, RandomShadow, ISONoise and motion and defocus blur, but it is dual-licensed AGPL-3.0 or commercial ([AlbumentationsX](https://github.com/albumentations-team/AlbumentationsX)). AugLy is MIT but has no perspective transform ([AugLy](https://github.com/facebookresearch/AugLy)). Build the simulator on kornia, AugLy or the last MIT release of albumentations. The ranges the plan starts from are my estimates, not published values, so the simulator step tunes them against gold-photo statistics: paste onto backgrounds at 55 to 95% fill, corner jitter of 0.03 to 0.12, rotation within ±15°, glare blobs over 2 to 25% of the area, white-balance gains of 0.85 to 1.15, defocus and motion blur, hand occlusion up to 20%, sleeve wear and price stickers.

Distillation then moves the trained teacher into a phone-sized student, and two published patterns transfer. MobileCLIP precomputes teacher outputs once into a "reinforced" dataset, so student training never runs the teacher, and its final loss is pure distillation: a KL divergence between teacher and student similarity distributions ([MobileCLIP2](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.20691v1)). RDCD distilled a ResNet-50 copy detector into EfficientNet-B0 and gained 5.0 to 5.9 µAP at 64 to 256 dims, and its 64-d descriptor (53.5 µAP_SN) nearly matched a 1536-d DINO ViT-B/16 (53.8) ([RDCD](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.17928)). For Record Scan, the student should regress its photo embedding onto the teacher's clean-cover embedding, with an InfoNCE term over a bank of precomputed teacher gallery vectors. That is asymmetric retrieval. The gallery stays in teacher space on the server or in the on-device codes, only the query runs the student, and a new student never forces a re-embed of 3.13M covers. As a rough guide to retention, DINOv3's own students keep about 82% (ViT-S) and 96% (ViT-B) of the 7B teacher's Oxford-Hard score ([DINOv3](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.10104v1)). How much a cover-specific student keeps is unmeasured.

The compute fits a hobby budget, though these figures are my estimates and need a one-hour pilot to confirm. RunPod lists H100 80GB from $1.99/hr, A100 80GB from $1.19/hr and RTX 4090 from $0.34/hr ([RunPod pricing](https://www.runpod.io/gpu-cloud/pricing)). Vast.ai lists the 4090 at about $0.14 to $0.50/hr ([Hostinger comparison](https://www.hostinger.com/tutorials/?p=158956)), and Modal's Starter plan includes $30 of compute a month ([Modal pricing](https://modal.com/pricing)). By my estimate, embedding 3.13M covers with a ViT-B or ViT-L teacher takes under an hour on one H100 or 4090, a few dollars. A teacher fine-tune over 10M to 30M samples takes 5 to 20 GPU hours, $10 to $60. Student distillation with precomputed targets takes 3 to 10 hours on a 4090, $2 to $15. The real bottleneck is data movement: 250 GB of JPEGs on a USB drive. Resized to 256 px and packed into WebDataset shards, they shrink to an estimated 50 to 80 GB, which uploads in 3 to 7 hours at 20 to 50 Mbit/s. The M1 with 64 GB runs the evaluation, faiss over 3.13M x 256-d vectors (3.2 GB at fp32, my arithmetic), linear heads on cached features, 50k to 200k-cover ablations and Core ML export. It is the wrong machine for full fine-tunes.

## Embed on the phone, search on a server until product quantization proves itself

Embedding is not the latency problem on either platform. On the Qualcomm AI Hub, CLIP ViT-B/16 at 224 px runs in **10.4 to 13.2 ms on a Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5, 14 to 17 ms on an 8 Elite, 17 to 21 ms on an 8 Gen 3 and 37 to 41 ms on a mid-range 7 Gen 4** ([Qualcomm AI Hub](https://huggingface.co/qualcomm/OpenAI-Clip)). Apple reports 10.4 ms for the ViT-B-sized MobileCLIP-B on a 2020 iPhone 12 Pro Max, and 1.5 to 3.6 ms for S0 and S2 ([apple/MobileCLIP2-S0](https://huggingface.co/apple/MobileCLIP2-S0)). Nobody has published phone latency for DINOv3, SigLIP 2 or PE, or for any ViT-S/16, so the project must measure its own. Non-Snapdragon Android (Tensor, Exynos) has no published numbers at all. Two practical risks matter more than milliseconds. The iPhone 18 Pro's first Neural Engine load can take minutes ([Apple Developer Forums 850058](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/850058)), so the app must compile and cache the model at first launch. And RoPE and register tokens in DINOv3 ViTs are untested conversion territory, which makes ConvNeXt-T worth carrying as a student candidate.

The index is where on-device gets hard. The table uses N = 3,128,310 and my arithmetic. The graph row assumes M=16 at about 0.44 GB.

| Encoding | Bytes per vector | 3.13M vectors | Fits a phone download? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 768-d fp32 (raw DINOv3-B) | 3,072 | 9.61 GB | no |
| 256-d fp32 + HNSW graph | 1,024 + ~140 | ~3.6 GB | no; fine on a 16 GB server |
| 256-d fp16 | 512 | 1.60 GB | poorly |
| 256-d int8 | 256 | 0.80 GB | poorly |
| IVF-PQ64 + 8 B id | 72 | 0.23 GB + 17 MB centroids | yes |
| IVF-PQ32 + 8 B id | 40 | 0.13 GB + 17 MB centroids | yes |

On SIFT1M, IVF-PQ reaches recall@1 of about 0.62 against 0.98 for HNSW ([Vearch evaluation](https://vearch.readthedocs.io/en/latest/evaluation.html)). Near-duplicate cover matching puts the true match much farther ahead of its neighbors than SIFT queries do, so the loss should be smaller. Whether it is small enough is **the single most important number for the on-device decision**, and only the gold set can measure it. Shipping is possible either way. A 0.2 GB index fits one Google Play asset pack (1.5 GB cap) and one Apple-hosted Background Assets pack ([Google Play Console help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9302563?hl=ur); [App Store Connect](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/apple-hosted-asset-pack-size-limits)). Among on-device search libraries, USearch has Swift and Java bindings, int8 and 1-bit storage and memory-mapped serving ([USearch](https://github.com/unum-cloud/usearch)). Faiss is the only one with IVF-PQ and OPQ, but needs a hand-written bridge. sqlite-vec's brute-force scan extrapolates to about 2 s per query at this scale (my arithmetic from 67.84 ms per 100k 384-d vectors) and is out ([Bambini](https://marcobambini.substack.com/p/the-state-of-vector-search-in-sqlite)). None of these libraries publishes a phone benchmark at million scale.

The server side is small and cheap. A 256-d float index with its graph needs about 3.6 GB of RAM. A Hetzner CAX31 (8 ARM vCPU, 16 GB) costs €20.99 a month after the June 2026 increase ([wz-it.com](https://wz-it.com/blog/hetzner-cloud-oder-dedicated-server/)), though sources conflicted in August 2026 on whether it can be ordered ([Findstack](https://findstack.com/resources/hetzner-price-increase-2026); [Sparecores](https://sparecores.com/server/hcloud/cax31)). Cloudflare Vectorize works out to about $22 a month at 100,000 queries for 3.13M 512-d vectors, by my arithmetic from its pricing ([Cloudflare Vectorize pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/vectorize/platform/pricing/)), and a 256-d index halves both the storage and query terms. Faiss HNSW answers SIFT1M queries in 0.04 to 0.1 ms at R@1 of 0.97 to 0.99 ([faiss wiki](https://github.com/facebookresearch/faiss/wiki/Indexing-1M-vectors)), so the mobile network, not search, sets the latency. My estimated hybrid budget is 5 to 15 ms to crop, 10 to 40 ms to embed, 30 to 150 ms of network and 1 to 5 ms of search: 50 to 200 ms per identification. That is near realtime if the app sends a request when the crop is stable rather than every frame. Fully offline buys less than it seems, because the Deezer preview needs the network anyway.

Query framing decides accuracy more than any detector. Meivar et al. tested 12 backbones, including DINOv3-L and SigLIP-2-L, and found that **every one degrades as the object fills a different share of the query than of the gallery image, losing 0.5 to 0.9 R@1 at the extremes. Resolution explains only about 19% of DINOv3-L's drop** ([arXiv 2610.11489](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.11489v1)). The failure is asymmetric: a query whose object is larger than the gallery's matches more reliably than the reverse. Cover Art Archive images are full-bleed, the tightest gallery possible, so a loose phone frame is the hard direction, and a square framing guide that the user fills turns it into the easy one. On ILIAS 100M, gray-padded 5-scale query averaging lifted DINOv3-L R@1 from 55.0 to 58.2, and a LoRA fine-tune reached 60.2 in one forward pass ([arXiv 2610.11489](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.11489v1)). Their OWLv2 re-ranking reached 76.0, but most of that gain came from cropping objects inside cluttered gallery images, which covers do not have. The authors note that cropping the query to its box performs about the same as gray-pad averaging. The cheap stack is therefore the overlay crop, 2 or 3 query scales, and a rectangle refinement where the platform offers one. On iOS, VNDetectRectanglesRequest returns corner points and accepts aspect-ratio limits, with defaults of 0.5 to 1.0 and a minimum size of 0.2 ([objc2-vision mirror of Apple headers](https://docs.rs/objc2-vision/0.3.2/objc2_vision/struct.VNDetectRectanglesRequest.html)). Android has no first-party live corner detector, because the ML Kit document scanner is a closed Play-services flow ([ML Kit doc scanner](https://developers.google.com/ml-kit/vision/doc-scanner)), so OpenCV contours fill that role. A trained detector is the last resort. YOLO26n runs at 3.7 ms on an iPhone 17 Pro ([Ultralytics CoreML docs](https://docs.ultralytics.com/integrations/coreml)), but Ultralytics recommends at least 1,500 images per class ([Ultralytics training tips](https://docs.ultralytics.com/yolov5/tutorials/tips_for_best_training_results/)), and the old annotations are gone.

## The plan: twelve gated steps from gold set to phone

Each step names its work, the machine, what it measures, and the decision that measurement drives. Steps 0 to 4 run on the M1 and Gautham's phones and cost nothing. Steps 5, 8 and 9 rent a GPU. The thresholds are my judgment, chosen so that a decision rests on a difference larger than gold-set noise. With 300 positive photos, a 95% confidence interval on an 80% recall is about ±4.5 points; with 40 it is ±12 (my arithmetic, binomial). Compare two models on the same photos by counting the photos where they disagree, which separates real gains from noise better than comparing totals. Several photos of the same sleeve are correlated, so per-sleeve recall is the stricter report. The steps refine the existing recovery plan: Step 2 replaces the D3 candidate list in Task 9, Step 5 changes how Task 10 runs, Step 1's dedup absorbs T15, and Step 10 is T16 ([recovery plan](../../superpowers/plans/2026-10-24-record-scan-recovery.md)).

| Step | Work | Where | Measures | Decision it drives |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Gold set and eval upgrades | phone, M1 | nothing yet; enables all later numbers | none |
| 1 | Copy, shard, pHash, dedup gallery | M1 + drive | cluster count, deduplicated N | index size; negatives for training |
| 2 | Frozen backbone sweep, 50k distractors | M1 | lenient R@1, R@5, per-slice recall | shortlist of 2 teachers, 2 students; skip-training check |
| 3 | Query crop study | M1, iPhone | R@1 by crop method; failure attribution | framing-only vs rectangle refinement vs detector |
| 4 | Export spike | M1, phones | latency, first-load time, embedding parity | which students are shippable |
| 5 | Full-gallery frozen eval and first server index | rented GPU, M1 | R@1 drop from 50k to full; server latency | teacher choice; hybrid v1 goes live |
| 6 | Photo simulator, calibrated | M1 | synthetic vs real recall gap | simulator ranges frozen |
| 7 | Projection head on frozen features | M1 | R@1 gain; loss from 768 to 256-d | dimension; whether full fine-tune is needed |
| 8 | Teacher fine-tune (conditional) | rented GPU | gold R@1 vs Step 7 | adopt fine-tuned teacher or not |
| 9 | Student distillation | rented GPU | student R@1 retention, on-device latency | which student ships |
| 10 | Index compression study | M1 | R@1 of PQ and binary vs exact search | on-device index or stay hybrid |
| 11 | App integration and lock calibration | phones | lock precision, time to lock | lock threshold and frame count |

### Step 0: build a gold set large enough to make decisions

Photograph 60 to 100 of Gautham's own records, 3 to 5 photos each, for 300 positives. Vary the conditions deliberately: lamp and daylight, glare, 10 to 30° tilt, a hand on the sleeve, shelf backgrounds, shrink-wrapped and worn copies. The plan's current H2 target of 25 to 40 photos gives ±12-point intervals and can only catch a broken model. Use it for a first smoke run, then grow it. Add 100 to 200 negatives (a desk, shelf spines, a CD, a book, a screen) and a few records known to be missing from Cover Art Archive. Add an optional `mbid` column to `labels.csv` alongside `file,artist,title`. Extend `pipeline/retrieval/eval.py` to report lenient recall@1 and recall@5, where a hit is the same album or a near-duplicate cover. Pending release-group data, count a hit when pHash Hamming distance is 8 of 64 bits or less, a starting guess to tune by inspecting pairs. Report strict release accuracy too, but never gate on it, since identical sleeves make it unreachable. Also report µAP over positives plus negatives, which measures the calibration a lock threshold depends on ([SSCD](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.10261)), and per-slice recall for text-only, dark, glare and occluded photos. Build a 10k to 50k synthetic query set from a held-out augmentation seed for fast regression checks. The gold photos stay hold-outs and never enter training.

### Step 1: copy and deduplicate the gallery in one pass over the drive

Read every cover from the Seagate drive once, sequentially. In the same pass, copy each original cover unchanged into WebDataset tar shards (about 278 GB) and compute a 64-bit pHash per cover. Cluster exact and near-duplicate hashes, and record each cluster's member releases. Measure the cluster count and the deduplicated N. That N sets every index size in this report. If dedup cuts N by half, IVF-PQ64 drops to about 0.11 GB. The clusters also define the negatives to exclude in Steps 8 and 9. When the tar.xz re-slim adds `release-group.id`, merge by release group as a second key.

### Step 2: sweep frozen backbones with no index-time augmentation

Embed every gold target plus 50,000 seeded distractors once per model on the M1, at one vector per image. Query with the overlay crop at 3 scales: the crop, a 10% tighter crop and a gray-padded crop, max-pooling the scores. Candidates are CLIP ViT-B/32 as the baseline, DINOv3 ViT-S/16, ViT-S+/16, ConvNeXt-T and ViT-B/16, SigLIP 2 B/16 at 256 and 384 px, and PE-Core S/16. Add MobileCLIP2-S0 and S2 only after someone reads Apple's model terms. Run the DINOv3 models at both 224 and 448 px to measure resolution sensitivity. Record M1 images per second for each model, because those numbers price Step 5. The step produces a shortlist: the two best models of any size become teacher candidates, and the two best at 30M parameters or less become student candidates. It also checks whether training is needed at all. If a frozen student candidate reaches at least 90% lenient R@1 and 97% R@5 at 50k distractors, skip Steps 7 to 9 for now and ship it in Step 5's hybrid. Fall back to training only if the full-gallery result in Step 5 drops below that bar.

### Step 3: decide whether framing alone handles the crop

On the gold set, compare four query treatments with the best Step 2 model: the plain overlay crop, the 3-scale average, VNDetectRectanglesRequest perspective correction (minimum aspect ratio about 0.85 and minimum size about 0.4, both untested starting points) falling back to the overlay when confidence is low, and an OpenCV contour pass for Android. Label each miss by cause: framing or skew, glare, text-only cover, cover not in the gallery, or a wrong near-duplicate. If crop and skew account for less than a quarter of the misses left after the best treatment, close the detector track for good. If they account for more, Step 6 adds more perspective range first. Revive the YOLO26n path only if that also fails: auto-label a few hundred photos with OWLv2, hand-correct them, and train a one-class oriented-box or 4-keypoint model.

### Step 4: export the student candidates before training them

Convert the Step 2 student candidates to Core ML with coremltools 9, and to Android through ExecuTorch with XNNPACK and the Qualcomm backend, or through LiteRT. Measure median and p95 latency at 224 and 256 px on each available phone, cold first-load time, and peak memory. Also check parity: the cosine between Mac and device embeddings of the same image should be at least 0.99. A candidate that fails conversion, misses parity, or exceeds 30 ms p95 on the oldest target phone drops out before any GPU money is spent on it. This step is cheap and kills the RoPE conversion risk early.

### Step 5: embed the full gallery and stand up hybrid v1

Upload the Step 1 shards to a rented GPU once. Embed the deduplicated gallery with both teacher candidates, an estimated hour or less per model on a 4090 or H100. Rerun the gold eval against the full gallery. The model with the higher full-gallery lenient R@1 becomes the teacher. Its drop from 50k distractors is the distractor penalty, and a drop of more than 5 points means hard negatives are hurting and Step 8 should mine them. Build a faiss HNSW index at the teacher's native dimension (768-d fp32 is 9.6 GB, which the 64 GB Mac holds), and swap in the 256-d version when Step 7 lands. Serve it from the existing FastAPI `/identify` on the Mac. In v1 the phone uploads the crop as a JPEG and the server runs the teacher, so the app works before any student exists. Measure server query latency and end-to-end time from the phone.

### Step 6: build the photo simulator and calibrate it against real photos

Implement the augmentation pipeline from the previous section on kornia, AugLy or a pinned MIT release of albumentations. Calibrate it by comparing simple statistics between synthetic and gold photos: blur level, mean brightness, glare area, skew angle, cover fill fraction. Then run the Step 5 teacher on a synthetic query set and on the gold set. The simulator is good enough when synthetic recall lands within 5 points of gold recall and the per-slice failure ranking matches. If synthetic recall is far higher than gold recall, the simulator is too easy. Widen glare, perspective and background clutter until the gap closes. A Blender render of bent, glossy sleeves under HDRI light is the escalation if 2D compositing plateaus.

### Step 7: train a projection head on cached frozen features

On the M1, cache teacher features for 100k to 200k covers and their synthetic photos. Train GeM pooling plus a linear projection to 256-d with asymmetric InfoNCE and KoLeo (λ = 30, τ = 0.05, with a memory bank), then learn PCA whitening on the gallery. Measure gold R@1 at 768, 512, 256 and 128 dimensions. Choose the smallest dimension within 1 point of the best. The gain over the frozen teacher decides Step 8. If the head adds 5 points or more, there is signal a full fine-tune can push further, so run Step 8. If it adds under 2 points, skip Step 8 and distill from the frozen teacher plus head. Expect a larger gain on SigLIP 2 than on DINOv3, matching ILIAS.

### Step 8: fine-tune the teacher only if the head showed headroom

On a rented A100 or H100, fine-tune the chosen teacher SSCD-style on the full deduplicated gallery. Use asymmetric clean-to-synthetic pairs, KoLeo, a memory bank, teacher-mined hard negatives with same-cluster items excluded, and the GUIE two-phase schedule of head first, then backbone with the head frozen. Budget 10M to 30M samples, an estimated $10 to $60. Adopt the fine-tuned teacher only if it beats Step 7 by at least 3 points of lenient R@1 on the gold set and loses no slice by more than 3 points. Otherwise keep the Step 7 teacher. Adopting it means re-embedding the gallery, an estimated hour.

### Step 9: distill the phone student against precomputed teacher targets

Store teacher gallery embeddings once: 3.13M x 256 fp16 is 1.6 GB (my arithmetic). Train each surviving Step 4 student on synthetic photos with three losses: cosine regression of the student's photo embedding onto the teacher's clean-cover embedding, InfoNCE against a bank of teacher gallery vectors, and an optional KL term on in-batch similarity distributions. Budget an estimated 3 to 10 hours on one RTX 4090, $2 to $15. Use 16 or more vCPUs, or GPU augmentation, because CPU augmentation will be the bottleneck. Ship the smallest student that keeps lenient R@1 within 2 points of the teacher on the gold set, querying the teacher-space gallery, and stays under 30 ms p95 on the oldest target phone. Hybrid v2 then sends the 512-byte vector instead of the JPEG, and the photo never leaves the phone.

### Step 10: test whether the index can move onto the phone

On the M1, build faiss IVF-PQ64, IVF-PQ32, OPQ+PQ64 and binary-plus-rescore indexes over the final gallery vectors, and query them with the Step 9 student on the gold set. If IVF-PQ64 stays within 2 points of exact-search lenient R@1, ship it as a Background Assets pack on iOS and a fast-follow Play asset pack on Android, about 0.11 to 0.23 GB depending on Step 1's N. Keep the server as fallback until the pack lands and for index updates. If PQ64 loses more, stay hybrid. Then measure on-device query latency and memory at full scale with USearch or a faiss bridge, because no public benchmark exists. Shard the index by IVF list so new releases do not force a full re-download.

### Step 11: calibrate the lock and integrate the app

On the gold positives plus negatives, pick the cosine threshold on the normalized top-1 score that gives 99% precision on accepted locks. Add a margin test between the top-1 score and the best score from a different cover cluster. Report recall at that operating point. Require 3 consecutive agreeing frames before locking, then tune 3 to 5 against time-to-lock on video. Route low-margin results to on-device OCR matched against artist and title, the fallback for text-only covers. My suggested ship bar: 99% lock precision, at least 85% of gold positives locked within 3 seconds, and a blank wall that never locks.

### What nobody has measured yet

The plan carries five unknowns, and each has a step that resolves it. No published DINOv3 retrieval exists at 224 px or for ConvNeXt (Step 2). No phone latency is published for DINOv3, SigLIP 2, PE or any ViT-S, or for any model on Tensor or Exynos Android (Step 4). Product quantization's recall on near-duplicate art is unmeasured (Step 10). MobileCLIP2's license is unread (before Step 2). The GPU hour figures are estimates that a 1,000-step pilot must replace before Step 8 is budgeted. The DINOv3 Table 9 row mapping came through a fetch tool and should be checked against the PDF before anyone quotes it outside this report.

## Conclusion

The November 2025 design spent its effort in the wrong place. It made the index carry tolerance to phone conditions by storing 13.6 views of every cover, which multiplied memory by 13.6 and left the query encoder untrained. The research reverses that. The gallery is the easy side, clean and full-bleed, and one vector per deduplicated cover is enough. The query is the hard side, and every lever that works acts on it: the framing guide, multi-scale query views, the photo simulator, asymmetric training and distillation. That reversal is also what makes on-device search plausible. Once the vectors are 256-d and one per cover, the index fits the 0.1 to 0.2 GB range a phone can download.

The binding constraint is no longer compute or model choice. It is evidence. The total GPU bill for the plan is likely under $100, and the candidate models are free to ship with a license file. What the project lacks is 300 labeled phone photos. Every gate in the plan, from teacher choice to whether a detector or a trained teacher or an on-device index is needed, reads its answer from that set. The highest-value hour Gautham can spend next is photographing records, before any agent writes training code.
