# EmbeddingGemma 2 earns one row in the backbone sweep, and no text embedder earns a job

EmbeddingGemma 2 is real. Google released it on 6 October 2026 under Apache 2.0, and it is a multimodal model, not the text-only model its name suggests ([launch blog](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/embeddinggemma-2); [HF card](https://huggingface.co/google/embeddinggemma-2)). Its value to Record Scan is on the image side. On RP2k, a set of retail products photographed on store shelves, it finds the right product first 73.7% of the time, ahead of the three other omni models scored on the same dataset revision, which run up to 7B parameters. Its image embeddings also read printed text, which DINOv2 cannot do at all. Text-only covers are the slice the existing report expects DINOv3 to miss, so EmbeddingGemma 2 should join Task 7's frozen sweep as an eighth embedder and a teacher candidate. It cannot be the phone student. At its smallest image budget it takes 49 to 70 ms per image on 2026 flagship phones and downloads at 388 MB, against the plan's 30 ms bar and 20 to 30M parameters. As a text model it has no job. The OCR fallback matches noisy proper names against the artist and title strings of the catalog's 3.97M releases, and a trigram index on the existing SQLite catalog does that with no model. The other tiny text embedders of 2025 and 2026 (Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B, Granite R2, LFM2.5, jina v5) do not change that answer.

## EmbeddingGemma 2 is a 440M text and image model with Apache weights

The card describes one 768-d space for text, images, video and audio, built on Gemma 4. The full model has 740M parameters: a 270M text model (130M transformer plus 140M token embeddings), a 170M vision encoder and a 300M audio encoder. The encoders load separately, so a text and image deployment is 440M ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/google/embeddinggemma-2)). Matryoshka truncation is trained at 512, 256 and 128 dimensions. Google calls quality "close to lossless down to 256 dimensions" and warns that 128 "degrades multimodal quality substantially": MIEB-lite falls from 64.64 at 768-d to 63.13 at 256-d and 59.06 at 128-d ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/google/embeddinggemma-2)). An image costs 70 to 1,120 soft tokens, 280 by default ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/google/embeddinggemma-2)). Each soft token pools a 3x3 block of 16 px patches ([processor_config.json](https://huggingface.co/google/embeddinggemma-2/blob/main/processor_config.json)), so by my arithmetic the 70-token budget covers about 400 px square and the default about 800 px.

The license metadata and the linked license page are Apache 2.0, and the card adds that deployments "must adhere to the Gemma Prohibited Use Policy" ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/google/embeddinggemma-2); [Gemma 4 license](https://ai.google.dev/gemma/docs/gemma_4_license)). That is simpler than the DINOv3 License the current plan leans on, and simpler than EmbeddingGemma 1, which ships under the gated Gemma terms ([google/embeddinggemma-300m](https://huggingface.co/google/embeddinggemma-300m)). No paper exists for version 2. The only EmbeddingGemma paper on arXiv describes the 300M text model ([arXiv 2509.20354](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.20354)), and the launch blog links none ([launch blog](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/embeddinggemma-2)). Every number below comes from model cards and the MTEB results repository.

The repo can load it today. Its `transformers==5.19.0` pin already includes the `embedding_gemma2` model code ([transformers v5.19.0](https://github.com/huggingface/transformers/tree/v5.19.0/src/transformers/models/embedding_gemma2)).

## Its image embeddings read text, which is the slice DINOv3 is expected to miss

The published image numbers are thin but point the right way. No 2026 model appears on ILIAS ([ILIAS](https://vrg.fel.cvut.cz/ilias/)), and the MTEB results repository holds no DINOv3 or SigLIP 2 runs, so there is no direct comparison with the plan's favorites. What exists:

- **Retail shelf photos.** RP2k holds over 500,000 photos of 2,000 products "captured manually in physical retail stores with natural lightings" ([arXiv 2006.12634](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.12634)). That is the closest public proxy to a phone photo of a sleeve in a record store. EmbeddingGemma 2 scores 73.7 hit@1 ([result](https://github.com/embeddings-benchmark/results/blob/main/results/google__embeddinggemma-2/914f7f89142e33e77833254d9c9b90c3cef7303b/RP2kI2IRetrieval.json)), against 56.7 for jina-embeddings-v5-omni-small at 1.6B ([result](https://github.com/embeddings-benchmark/results/blob/main/results/jinaai__jina-embeddings-v5-omni-small/dfdcc361ec47c69a5afcd81e4bd148abb9d0568e/RP2kI2IRetrieval.json)), 53.6 for LCO-Embedding-Omni-7B ([result](https://github.com/embeddings-benchmark/results/blob/main/results/LCO-Embedding__LCO-Embedding-Omni-7B/3d38f58aae1253a4443b1270b0767f1e533936cf/RP2kI2IRetrieval.json)) and 48.9 for jina's nano ([result](https://github.com/embeddings-benchmark/results/blob/main/results/jinaai__jina-embeddings-v5-omni-nano/2b230c93c996e091a45b95af4e3315dd07605ee3/RP2kI2IRetrieval.json)). The DINOv2, SigLIP and CLIP rows were run on an older revision of the dataset, where each query also matched itself (precision@1 of 0.9998), so they are not comparable ([DINOv2-S result](https://github.com/embeddings-benchmark/results/blob/main/results/facebook__dinov2-small/ed25f3a31f01632728cabb09d1542f84ab7b0056/RP2kI2IRetrieval.json)).
- **Fine-grained birds.** CUB-200 is on the same dataset revision for all of them, though scored by different mteb versions. There DINOv2 still wins: 83.5 for DINOv2-S and 85.7 for DINOv2-B, against 78.7 for EmbeddingGemma 2, 67.2 for SigLIP B/16 and 52.1 for the CLIP ViT-B/32 the project started from ([EG2](https://github.com/embeddings-benchmark/results/blob/main/results/google__embeddinggemma-2/914f7f89142e33e77833254d9c9b90c3cef7303b/CUB200I2IRetrieval.json); [DINOv2-S](https://github.com/embeddings-benchmark/results/blob/main/results/facebook__dinov2-small/ed25f3a31f01632728cabb09d1542f84ab7b0056/CUB200I2IRetrieval.json); [DINOv2-B](https://github.com/embeddings-benchmark/results/blob/main/results/facebook__dinov2-base/f9e44c814b77203eaa57a6bdbbd535f21ede1415/CUB200I2IRetrieval.json); [SigLIP B](https://github.com/embeddings-benchmark/results/blob/main/results/google__siglip-base-patch16-256/b078df89e446d623010d890864d4207fe6399f61/CUB200I2IRetrieval.json); [CLIP B/32](https://github.com/embeddings-benchmark/results/blob/main/results/openai__clip-vit-base-patch32/3d74acf9a28c67741b2f4f2ea7635f0aaf6f0268/CUB200I2IRetrieval.json)). Self-supervised features still lead on pure visual detail.
- **Printed text.** VisualSTS renders sentence pairs as images and asks whether the image embeddings know the sentences mean the same thing. On STS15 EmbeddingGemma 2 scores 85.7, SigLIP B/16 74.3, CLIP B/32 59.6, and DINOv2-S and DINOv2-B 12.0 and 13.8 ([EG2](https://github.com/embeddings-benchmark/results/blob/main/results/google__embeddinggemma-2/914f7f89142e33e77833254d9c9b90c3cef7303b/STS15VisualSTS.json); [SigLIP B](https://github.com/embeddings-benchmark/results/blob/main/results/google__siglip-base-patch16-256/b078df89e446d623010d890864d4207fe6399f61/STS15VisualSTS.json); [CLIP B/32](https://github.com/embeddings-benchmark/results/blob/main/results/openai__clip-vit-base-patch32/3d74acf9a28c67741b2f4f2ea7635f0aaf6f0268/STS15VisualSTS.json); [DINOv2-S](https://github.com/embeddings-benchmark/results/blob/main/results/facebook__dinov2-small/ed25f3a31f01632728cabb09d1542f84ab7b0056/STS15VisualSTS.json); [DINOv2-B](https://github.com/embeddings-benchmark/results/blob/main/results/facebook__dinov2-base/f9e44c814b77203eaa57a6bdbbd535f21ede1415/STS15VisualSTS.json)). The DINOv2 rows use an older dataset revision, but a gap of 70 points is not a revision artifact. It also scores 67.84 NDCG@5 on MMEB's visual document retrieval, where text queries find pages by their printed content ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/google/embeddinggemma-2)).

My reading: DINOv3 shares DINOv2's text-free training, so it will likely share the blindness to type. That is an inference, since no VisualSTS run of DINOv3 exists. A text-only cover in a crowded gallery is a test of reading, and EmbeddingGemma 2 reads better than the VLMs already in the sweep. Product photos are its best published result. The sweep, not these benchmarks, decides whether it beats DINOv3 on covers. But it is the strongest candidate for the slice the existing report singled out.

## It is too heavy to be the phone student

The LiteRT Community builds that the launch blog points to publish phone numbers for the text and image model at the 70-token image budget. One image takes 49 ms on a Pixel 11 Pro TPU, 69.8 ms on an iPhone 18 Pro GPU, 119 ms on an S26 Ultra GPU and 191 ms on the iPhone 18 Pro CPU. The download is 388 MB, with int4 weights for the text transformer and int8 for the vision encoder ([LiteRT text-vision card](https://huggingface.co/litert-community/embeddinggemma-2-text-vision-440m-litert-lm)). The plan's student budget is 20 to 30M parameters under 30 ms p95 on the oldest target phone. EmbeddingGemma 2 misses it on the newest phones.

Two further problems apply on iOS. Google warns that float16 "returns NaN or silently degraded embeddings" because the activations exceed its range ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/google/embeddinggemma-2)), and the Neural Engine runs float16. The community Core ML conversion keeps text on the Neural Engine but runs images on the GPU ([FluidInference Core ML](https://huggingface.co/FluidInference/embeddinggemma-2-coreml)). An Apple Neural Engine port reports about 450 ms per image end to end at the default 280 tokens on an M4 Pro ([anemll ANE](https://huggingface.co/anemll/anemll-embeddinggemma-2-ane)).

The server side is workable but not free. LiteRT reports 825 ms per image on an Arm CPU and 23.9 ms on an RTX 4090 ([LiteRT text-vision card](https://huggingface.co/litert-community/embeddinggemma-2-text-vision-440m-litert-lm)). That is fine as a teacher, which embeds the gallery once on a rented GPU and is then distilled into the student. It is slow as the CPU-server query encoder for crate flipping at 2 frames a second.

## No text embedder earns a place in the identifier

The obvious text jobs are the Task 17 OCR fallback, which matches the artist and title visible on a sleeve against the catalog, and the labeler's search box, which already uses `Catalog.search`. Neither needs embeddings. Both match proper names: band names, album titles, often invented words. Dense retrievers "drastically underperform sparse methods" on entity-centric questions ([Sciavolino et al., EMNLP 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.08535)), and OCR noise turns a name into fragments, not into a paraphrase. The catalog already has an FTS5 table over title and artist names (plan, Task 1). An FTS5 trigram tokenizer adds substring matching on any run of 3 or more characters ([SQLite FTS5](https://www.sqlite.org/fts5.html#the_trigram_tokenizer)), which absorbs a dropped letter or a cut-off word with no model, no vectors and no download. A semantic model would help only if users typed descriptions ("the album with the prism"), and that is a cover search, which the image index already answers.

If a text model is ever needed, Granite 97M R2 is the one to try first: Apache 2.0, 97M parameters, and by IBM's account the best open multilingual retrieval score under 100M. I did not re-check the older small models (BGE, Snowflake Arctic, Nomic). None of them is multimodal, so none changes the image question.

Among tiny image and text models, nothing else since the existing report changes its backbone list. jina v5 omni is non-commercial and scores lower on RP2k. LCO-Embedding-Omni starts at 3B. MobileCLIP2, SigLIP 2 and PE-Core are already covered there.

## What to change in the plan

1. **Task 7, Step 2 sweep: add `embeddinggemma-2` as an eighth embedder.** Load the text and vision encoders only, in float32 like the other seven, never float16. Run it at 70 tokens, the budget a phone would use, as its own model name (`embeddinggemma-2-t70`) so the budget reaches the embedder signature. Skip the 280-token default: it needs about 800 px of cover, and Cover Art Archive covers top out at 500 px, so the gallery side would be upscaled. Report the `text-only` and `crate` slices beside the overall r1. My estimate from LiteRT's 37.3 ms per image on an M5 GPU ([LiteRT text-vision card](https://huggingface.co/litert-community/embeddinggemma-2-text-vision-440m-litert-lm)) is about 31 minutes for a 50,000-cover gallery; the M1 will be slower. Feed it the original covers, which the plan's sweep already reads. By my arithmetic a cover needs about 400 px square to fill 70 tokens, so the few smaller originals fill fewer.
2. **It reaches the shortlist only through the sweep's existing gate, and only as a teacher.** If it wins the text-only slice but misses the shortlist, take that to the Task 7 checkpoint with its record count rather than changing the gate. If it makes the shortlist, the Step 9 student distills from its gallery vectors at 256-d, which its Matryoshka training supports. Whether a 20 to 30M student keeps its text reading is the unmeasured risk. If it loses on every slice, drop it. A model that reads text but does not win on covers is not worth a second encoder.
3. **Task 17: build the OCR fallback on an FTS5 trigram table over title, artist names and aliases.** Add no embedding model.

One optional test costs an hour once step 1 has run. Embed `artist | title` for the gold set's releases with the text encoder, then score each `text-only` photo's image embedding against those strings. If the right album lands in the top 5 often, the shared space gives the app a text signal with no OCR step on either platform. Until that number exists, OCR plus trigram search stays the plan.
